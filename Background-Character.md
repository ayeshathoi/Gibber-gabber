I like being a background character.

As I grow older, I find myself missing the little moments I shared with my cadet college friends. Some would dance ridiculously while I stood beside them, singing completely nonsensical lyrics. There would be nice weather, silly conversations, and absolutely no reason to be anything other than ourselves. I miss that kind of ordinary happiness, where nothing had to mean anything beyond the fact that we were having a good time.

Even when I knew I could have been more than an ordinary Class 12 student, I deliberately chose to remain one from the very beginning. I remember my classmate, who was a house prefect, getting frustrated with me in Class 12. I don't remember exactly what prompted it, but she scolded me for not taking extracurricular activities more seriously. I don't know if she was unhappy with her group of prefects, but that wasn't my fault, and I couldn't solve the problem by becoming just another prefect. According to her, I could have easily become one if I had put in the effort. People were practically dying to become prefects, and there I was, doing nothing particularly seriously, simply because I didn't want to.

The same was true of my class teacher, who had known me for six years. When it came time to select prefects for my batch, he was genuinely upset because he couldn't select me as a prefect due to my irresponsible attitude toward everything other than academics. XD Although, come to think of it, this was the same teacher who named his daughter after a name I had suggested. So, perhaps it was no surprise. I never wanted authority over others; I wanted the freedom to deviate from what people expected of me. I didn't want my potential to become an obligation. Moreover, I didn't want to take responsibility for things that were unimportant to me because life was already very exhausting.

Perhaps that's why I found my undergraduate years even more exhausting. Everything felt like a performance, as if everyone had to be the main character in some never-ending competition. People competed for better grades, better partners, better appearances, and better social standing. Somehow, I became a main character without even trying, which was precisely what I never wanted. Even when I wasn't competing with anyone, people seemed to be competing with me, sometimes disguising that rivalry as friendship. It is particularly unpleasant to discover that a friendship you considered genuine might have been another space for quiet comparison. At least in cadet college, I knew whom I was close to and whom I wasn't. During my undergraduate years, those boundaries often felt much less clear.

But, yes, I do want to be remembered. I suppose that's where the contradiction lies. I've always enjoyed surprising people, perhaps even more than meeting their expectations. I still find it hilarious how shocked some of the people who disliked me in college were when I got selected for the army.

I want the freedom to be ordinary when I feel like it and extraordinary when I choose to be. I want to be remembered as someone who fell but found her way back to the things she wanted, someone who kept trying and, hopefully, left a little something behind in the lives of others.
